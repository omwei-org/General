Open Initiative for Trustworthy Autonomous Systems

OMWEI explores the foundations required for autonomous systems to operate with trust, accountability, and justified authority.

As intelligent systems move from generating information to taking actions in the real world, new models of trust and governance are required.

Explore OMWEI on GitHub

The Challenge

Traditional computing focused on whether systems could calculate correctly.

Security focused on protecting systems from unauthorized access.

Autonomous systems introduce a deeper question:

When a system can decide and act, what makes that action trustworthy?

OMWEI brings together researchers, engineers, organizations, and practitioners exploring the principles, architectures, and practices needed for trustworthy autonomy.

Principles

Functional over technological

Trustworthy autonomy should be defined by required properties and guarantees, not by a specific implementation technology.

Evidence over assumptions

Claims about autonomous systems should be supported by verifiable evidence and reproducible evaluation.

Separation of intelligence and authority

The ability to make decisions and the authority to create real-world effects are distinct concepts and should not be implicitly merged.

Open inquiry

No architecture should be accepted without rigorous examination, comparison, and challenge.

Community
